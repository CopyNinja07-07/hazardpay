Insurance for robot jobs, priced by first trying to break the robot.

Bolt is a sidewalk delivery robot. Before it takes a delivery, we rebuild the route in a physics simulator and throw problems at it: ice, a sloped driveway, a curb, someone bumping into it. How often it fails sets the insurance price. If it really fails on the day, we replay what happened to find the cause, and PayPal pays the claim.

How Hazard Pay works

1. A copy of the routeWe rebuild the delivery route in a physics simulator: the curb, the sloped driveway, the weather, the cargo, Bolt’s motors.
2. A price from 120 test daysBolt drives the route 120 times on different days. If it fails 17 times out of 100, the price covers that risk.
3. An AI that hunts for weak spotsGemini suggests ways Bolt could fail. We test each idea, keep only the ones that really break it, and shrink them to the simplest version.
4. Practise, then a strict checkBolt practises on its failures. The new version only goes live if it beats a fixed set of tests. A safer robot gets a lower price.
5. Claims by replayIf Bolt fails on the day, we replay that run, removing one thing at a time. Covered cause: PayPal pays out. Excluded cause: no payout.

Every number on this page comes from the simulator. Gemini suggests ideas and writes explanations; it never sets a price or decides a claim.
