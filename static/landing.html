Insurance for robot jobs, priced by trying to break the robot first.

Before a delivery robot takes a job, we crash-test it 120 times in a physics copy of that exact route. How often it fails becomes the price. If it really fails, we replay the run to find the cause, and PayPal pays the claim in seconds.

The problem
8:47 pm, the first snow of the year. Maya’s little bakery has one delivery left: a birthday cake for a seven-year-old. Her robot, Bolt, slides on the icy driveway into the road. The cake never arrives.
−$400

Robots now work on real sidewalks, steps and factory floors, and they fail. But nobody can insure a single robot job, because nobody can say how risky this robot, on this route, tonight really is. So the cover doesn’t exist, and small businesses like Maya’s carry the whole loss.

How it works

Don’t guess the risk. Measure it.

1

Rebuild the route

A MuJoCo physics copy of the real route: the slope, the curb, the weather odds. Describe it or upload a photo; Gemini builds it.

2

Crash-test it 120×

Every run is a different day: dry, rain, ice, a stranger bumping in. The failure rate is the risk.

3

An AI hunts weak spots

Gemini plays the villain: suggests how Bolt fails; we search, shrink and re-check it on 32 fresh runs.

4

Safer → cheaper

Bolt practises on its failures and ships only after 3 strict checks. A safer robot pays less.

5

Claims by replay

Replay the exact run, removing one thing at a time. Covered cause → PayPal payout. No paperwork.

PayPal, all the way down

Every robot job becomes two PayPal transactions.

Route→Physics test→Price→PayPal Order→Cover on→Delivery→Replay→PayPal Payout
# premium: the customer pays
POST /v2/checkout/orders   intent=CAPTURE
  PayPal-Request-Id   = policy hash   # idempotent
  reference_id        = policy HMAC signature
POST /v2/checkout/orders/{id}/capture

# claim: the robot owner is paid
POST /v1/payments/payouts
  sender_item_id      = claim id
# every event: verify-webhook-signature

The price is a PayPal Order the customer approves. The policy’s cryptographic signature is stamped on the order, so every payment traces back to the exact risk we measured. A covered failure triggers a PayPal Payout to the owner. Tested end to end on the PayPal sandbox, including a real $400 sandbox payout.

Why PayPal wins

A brand-new category of payments: robot jobs. Illustration: 1 million robot jobs a month × a $1.50 average premium = $1.5M a month of new volume through PayPal Orders, plus every claim paid out with PayPal Payouts.
